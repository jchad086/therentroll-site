What changed for Ontario landlords on September 21, 2026

Twenty-eight amendments to the Residential Tenancies Act took effect that day. Here are the ones that change how you serve notice, checked line by line against the Act itself.

Published September 10, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026 · Checked against e-Laws, currency date November 11, 2026

The changes come from two different statutes, not one. Most of what follows is Schedule 12 of the Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act, 2025, which is Bill 60. Several other provisions that took effect the same day come from Schedule 7 of a 2023 act, 2023, c. 10. Both were passed earlier and commenced together by order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council.

The N4 waiting period dropped from 14 days to 7

Until September 21, section 59 of the Act gave two different waiting periods: the 7th day after notice for a daily or weekly tenancy, and the 14th day for everything else, which covered most monthly tenancies. On September 21 that subsection was repealed and replaced with a single rule.

Before Sept 21, 202614 days

Monthly and yearly tenancies. Daily and weekly were already 7.

Since Sept 21, 20267 days

One waiting period for every tenancy type.

Residential Tenancies Act, 2006, s. 59 (1), as amended by 2025, c. 14, Sched. 12, s. 4. Read it on e-Laws.

The rest of section 59 is unchanged. The notice still has to set out the rent owed, and it is still void if the tenant pays what is owed before you apply to the Board.

N12 own-use compensation is waived with 120 days notice

Section 48.1 requires a landlord ending a tenancy for their own use to compensate the tenant one month's rent, or offer another unit. On September 21 a new subsection was added that removes that requirement in one specific situation. All three conditions have to be met:

  1. The notice is given on or after September 21, 2026, the day the amendment came into force.
  2. The termination date in the notice is at least 120 days after the notice is given.
  3. The termination date is the day a period of the tenancy ends, or the end of the fixed term.

Give less than 120 days notice and the one month's compensation still applies, as it did before.

The waiver is narrow. It covers only an N12 given because the landlord or a family member will move in. It does not apply to an N12 given on behalf of a purchaser, which still carries one month's compensation under section 49.1. And it does not apply to an N13 for demolition, conversion or repairs, which falls under separate compensation rules.

One more change came with it. Where the landlord or family member does not move in within 60 days of the termination date in the N12, the Act now presumes the notice was given in bad faith if the former tenant applies to the Board. The landlord can rebut that presumption at the hearing.

Residential Tenancies Act, 2006, s. 48.1 (2), as added by 2025, c. 14, Sched. 12, s. 2 (2). Purchaser's own use: s. 49.1. Occupancy presumption: LTB operational update, September 21, 2026.

The window to request a review is 15 days

This one took effect before September 21, which is why it is easy to miss. Section 209 gives 15 days from the issuance of a decision or order to ask the Board to review it, down from 30. The Board can still extend that time where it considers it just and appropriate. The shorter window applies to decisions and orders made on or after the day the amendment came into force.

Appeals to the Divisional Court are a separate route and still carry 30 days.

Residential Tenancies Act, 2006, s. 209 (3), as amended by 2025, c. 14, Sched. 12, s. 11.

Old versions of ten LTB forms are refused from November 30

The Board updated ten forms on September 21 to reflect these changes. Previous versions of those forms will no longer be accepted as of November 30, 2026. The updated forms are on the Tribunals Ontario website and portal.

  • Notices: N5, N6, N8, N12, N13
  • Landlord applications: L1, L2, L9, L10
  • Tenant application: T5

The N4 was also replaced on September 21 to reflect the new seven-day period.

LTB operational update, September 21, 2026. Current LTB forms.

What is not changing, despite what you may have read

Several widely shared posts said that fixed-term leases would stop automatically becoming month-to-month when the term ends. That was never in this legislation. Section 38 of the Act is untouched by Schedule 12, and it still deems an expired fixed-term tenancy renewed as a monthly tenancy on the same terms.

Rechecked on November 15, 2026: section 38 still reads as it did before September 21.

There are 28 changes in total

The changes above are the ones that alter day-to-day practice for most landlords. The full set also touches former tenant applications about notices given in bad faith, what a tenant may raise at a hearing on a non-payment application, and several administrative provisions. If you have a matter in front of the Board, the detail of those will matter more than the summary here.

How to check any of this yourself

Every statement on this page is taken from the consolidated Act on Ontario's e-Laws, which cites the amending act beside every changed provision.

  • Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 on e-Laws
  • Bill 60 at the Legislative Assembly of Ontario
  • Landlord and Tenant Board, for forms and practice directions

These amendments commenced on September 21, 2026, the day named by order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council. The Act keeps changing, so check the current text on e-Laws rather than relying on any summary, this one included.
